# Hay fever

This page is for patients.

Hay fever is an allergy to pollen. It is usually worse from late March to September, when there is more pollen in the air.

## Causes of hay fever

Pollen is a fine powder that comes from grass, trees and weeds. If you have hay fever, your body reacts to pollen as if it were harmful. This causes the symptoms.

## Symptoms of hay fever

Symptoms of hay fever include:

- sneezing and coughing
- a runny or blocked nose
- itchy, red or watery eyes
- an itchy throat, mouth, nose or ears
- a headache
- feeling tired

A cold usually goes away within 1 or 2 weeks. Hay fever can last for weeks or months.

## Things you can try

There is no cure for hay fever, but you can do things to ease your symptoms. You can:

- put a little petroleum jelly around your nostrils to trap pollen
- wear wraparound sunglasses when you go out
- shower and change your clothes after you have been outside
- keep windows and doors shut when you can
- stay inside when the pollen count is high
- vacuum often and dust with a damp cloth

## Help from a pharmacist

A pharmacist can tell you about treatments for hay fever, such as tablets, eye drops and nasal sprays. Some tablets can make you feel sleepy, so ask which kind suits you.

## When to see a GP

See a GP if:

- your symptoms do not get better after trying treatments from a pharmacist
- you also have a wheeze or feel short of breath

## Treatment from a GP

A GP may suggest a steroid nasal spray or a different tablet. If these do not help, they may refer you to a specialist for more tests.
