# Example medicine A: information for healthcare professionals

This page is for healthcare professionals only. Patients should read the [patient information for Example medicine A](/medicines/example-medicine-a).

## Indication

Example medicine A is a selective peripheral H1-receptor antagonist indicated for the symptomatic relief of seasonal allergic rhinitis in adults and adolescents aged 12 years and over, where first-line antihistamine therapy has provided an inadequate response.

## Pharmacodynamic properties

Example medicine A is a long-acting antagonist with high selectivity for peripheral H1 receptors. Onset of action occurs within 1 hour of oral administration, and the effect is maintained for at least 24 hours. At therapeutic doses, it has low penetration of the blood-brain barrier.

## Dosage and administration

<!-- protected -->
Adults and adolescents aged 12 years and over: one 10 mg tablet once daily. The maximum daily dose is 10 mg. Not recommended for children under 12 years. In moderate to severe renal impairment, reduce the dose to 5 mg once daily.
<!-- /protected -->

## Contraindications

Hypersensitivity to the active substance or to any of the excipients. Severe renal impairment with creatinine clearance below 10 ml/min.

## Special warnings and precautions

Use with caution in patients with predisposing factors for urinary retention, including spinal cord lesion and prostatic hyperplasia. Patients should be advised that somnolence may occur and that this may affect their ability to drive or operate machinery.

## Pregnancy and lactation

There are limited data on the use of Example medicine A in pregnant women. As a precaution, avoid use during pregnancy. Example medicine A is excreted in human milk, so a decision should be made whether to discontinue breastfeeding or to discontinue therapy.

## Drug interactions

No clinically relevant pharmacokinetic interactions have been identified with macrolide antibiotics, azole antifungals or H2-receptor antagonists. Concomitant use with alcohol or other CNS depressants may potentiate sedative effects and should be avoided.

## Undesirable effects

The most frequently reported adverse reactions in clinical studies were somnolence (6%), dry mouth (4%) and headache (3%). These were generally mild to moderate in severity and transient.

## Overdose

Symptoms of overdose may include drowsiness, tachycardia and headache. There is no specific antidote. Management should be supportive and symptomatic, with monitoring of cardiac function.

## Reporting adverse reactions

<!-- protected -->
Suspected adverse reactions should be reported through the [Yellow Card scheme](https://yellowcard.mhra.gov.uk).
