# Example medicine A

This page is for patients who have been prescribed Example medicine A.

## About Example medicine A

Example medicine A is a tablet used to treat the symptoms of hay fever, such as sneezing, a runny nose and itchy eyes. It is prescribed by a doctor when other treatments have not helped enough. It works by stopping your body from reacting to pollen in the usual way.

## How to take it

<!-- protected -->
Take one tablet once a day. Do not take more than one tablet in 24 hours. If you take too much, contact your doctor or call NHS 111 straight away.
<!-- /protected -->

The tablet should be swallowed whole with a drink of water. It can be taken with or without food. It should be taken at the same time each day, so that it becomes part of your routine.

If a dose is forgotten, take it as soon as you remember. If it is nearly time for your next dose, skip the missed dose. Never take 2 doses at the same time to make up for a missed one.

## Possible side effects

Like all medicines, Example medicine A can cause side effects, although not everybody gets them. Common side effects include:

- feeling sleepy
- a dry mouth
- a headache

These are usually mild and should pass within a few days. Do not drive or use tools or machines if you feel sleepy.

Stop taking the tablets and get help straight away if you have swelling of the face, lips or throat, or if you find it hard to breathe.

## Who should not take it

Example medicine A is not suitable for everyone. Tell your doctor before you start if you are pregnant or breastfeeding, or if you have kidney problems.

## Storing your tablets

Keep the tablets in the box they came in. Store them somewhere cool and dry, and out of the sight and reach of children.

## Reporting side effects

<!-- protected -->
If you get any side effects, talk to your doctor, pharmacist or nurse. This includes any side effects not listed in the leaflet that comes with your medicine. You can also report side effects directly through the [Yellow Card scheme](https://yellowcard.mhra.gov.uk). Reporting side effects helps provide more information about this medicine.
